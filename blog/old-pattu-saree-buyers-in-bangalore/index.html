Old Pattu Saree Buyers in Bangalore: What to Know Before You Sell

By Nakshatra Kanchipuram Society | 7 min read

In Karnataka and Tamil Nadu homes, a silk saree is simply a "pattu". Most families have several: a Mysore silk from a cousin's wedding, a Kanchi pattu from a housewarming, a heavy one kept aside for a daughter. Over time, many of these sarees stop being worn. If you are looking for old pattu saree buyers in Bangalore, this guide explains which pattu sarees have resale value, how the buying process works, and what to ask before you sell.

Which Pattu Sarees Can You Sell?

Almost any pure silk pattu saree can be sold, but some are valued more highly than others. The common ones we see in Bangalore homes are:

  • Kanchipuram pattu: heavy mulberry silk, often with pure zari borders and pallu. Usually the most valuable.
  • Mysore silk: lighter and smooth, with a fine zari border. Sarees from Karnataka Silk Industries Corporation (KSIC) use zari described as containing about 65% silver and 0.65% gold, according to Wikipedia's page on Mysore silk.
  • Dharmavaram and Arani silks: broad borders and rich pallus, often with good zari content.
  • Banarasi silks: brocade work with zari woven into the body and pallu.
  • Bridal and antique pattu sarees: heavy zari and older, often richer zari make these very valuable.

Art silk, polyester and blended sarees generally have no resale value as silk, even if they look similar.

How Old Pattu Sarees Are Valued

A fair buyer looks at two things separately: the silk and the zari.

  • The silk is valued on its purity, weight and condition.
  • The zari is valued on whether it is pure, 2G or tested zari, how much of it there is, and the day's silver rate.

Since silver prices change daily, there is no single fixed rate for pattu sarees. We explain the full method in our guide to old silk saree zari price per gram. To check your zari at home first, see how to check pure zari in silk saree.

How the Selling Process Works in Bangalore

  1. Get in touch. Call or WhatsApp the buyer, or send photos for a preliminary estimate.
  2. Book a doorstep visit. A good buyer will come to your home so you do not have to carry heavy sarees across the city.
  3. Evaluation in front of you. The expert checks the silk, the zari and the weave, and weighs each saree where you can see the scale.
  4. Itemised quote. You get a price for each saree and can choose to sell all, some or none.
  5. Payment on the spot. If you accept, you are paid immediately by cash or UPI.

Tips to Get a Better Price for Your Pattu Sarees

  • Sell together. Gather all your pattu sarees for one evaluation, including the ones you think are worthless.
  • Leave the zari intact. Cutting borders or pallus lowers the value.
  • Keep the blouse piece if it is attached. It is part of the saree's silk and zari.
  • Look for Mysore silk labels. Genuine KSIC sarees carry identification such as a hologram or code, which helps confirm authenticity.
  • Do not rush. A good buyer gives you time to think before you accept.
